## 0. The geography behind the records

| Fact | Detail | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Village | **Vyšná Pisaná** = Hungarian **Felsőhímes** (until 1899: **Felső-Piszana**) | Search records under *all three* spellings; pre-1899 vs post-1899 Hungarian name differs. |
| District / region | Svidník District, Prešov Region, NE Slovakia | Regional archive = **Štátny archív v Prešove**. |
| Historic county | **Sáros County (Sáros vármegye)**, Kingdom of Hungary, until 1918 | Pre-1918 records are **Hungarian-administered**; look under Sáros/Šariš, not "Slovakia." |
| Village character | Tiny Rusyn highland village (pop. 74 in 2024); first mentioned 1600 | Small village → births often registered at a **district registry / nearby seat**, not in-village. |

**On the 1904 vs 1905 date:** Hungarian **civil registration became mandatory on 1 Oct 1895**,
so a birth on **31 Dec 1904** would sit in the state civil register — and a birth at the very
end of a year being *registered in early 1905* is completely normal (registration lag). The
"1905 birth record" and the "b. 31 Dec 1904" are consistent, not contradictory — a useful point
to pre-empt if the consulate flags the mismatch.

Sources: [Vyšná Pisaná — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vy%C5%A1n%C3%A1_Pisan%C3%A1) ·
[Slovakia Civil Registration — FamilySearch](https://www.familysearch.org/en/wiki/Slovakia_Civil_Registration)

## TASK 1 — Passenger manifest (the identity bridge)

**Goal:** find the 1922 arrival that shows *last residence = Felső-Piszana/Vyšná Pisaná*,
*place of birth*, and *nearest relative left behind = Meller Pál* — the line that welds
"Shija Meller" to "Sam Miller."

### Free places to search (no subscription)
- **FamilySearch — "New York, Passenger Arrival Lists (Ellis Island), 1892–1925"**
  (collection **1368704**). Free with a free account. Name-indexed, ~25M people.
  → https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/collection/1368704
- **Statue of Liberty–Ellis Island Foundation** — free passenger search.
  → https://www.statueofliberty.org/discover/passenger-ship-search/
- **Steve Morse "One-Step"** — the power tool. The raw Ellis Island site is clumsy; Morse's
  forms search the same data far better.
  - White Form (Ellis Island 1892–1924): https://stevemorse.org/ellis/ellis.html
  - **Hamburg outbound lists** (1850–1934) — the *departure* side of the same voyage, useful
    to cross-confirm: https://stevemorse.org/ellis/passengers.php?mode=hamb

### Search recipe (anchor everything to **arrival 14 Sept 1922, from Hamburg**)
1. Surname `Miller`, blank first name, arrival `1922`, port `New York`.
2. No hit → retry `Meller`, then `Möller`, then `Miler` (index mis-keys the umlaut/vowel).
3. Still nothing → first name `Sam`, birth `1904 ±2`, arrival `1922`, `New York`, **no surname**.
4. Cross-check the **Hamburg outbound** list for a Sept 1922 sailing to NY carrying a Meller.

The underlying microfilm is **NARA T715** if you ever need the archival citation.

Sources: [FamilySearch collection 1368704](https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/collection/1368704) ·

## TASK 2 — Czechoslovak citizenship record

### 🟢 The free shortcut: 1930 census online (do this before emailing anyone)
Slovakia has put the **entire 1930 census (sčítacie hárky) online, free and unredacted**
(the 90-year data-protection period has expired) — **623,259 sheets** on the national culture
portal **Slovakiana.sk**. A **1930 census record is named by Slovak lawyers as acceptable proof
of Czechoslovak citizenship** — which is exactly the "bulletproof" document Task 2 wanted.

**How to find the Meller family's sheet:**
1. Go to **https://www.slovakiana.sk/kulturne-objekty?cat=16**
2. Search: `sčítací hárok Vyšná Pisaná` (also try `Felsőhímes`, `Felső-Piszana`).
3. **Keep "LEN ZDIGITALIZOVANÉ" checked** (digitized only); **UNCHECK "VOĽNÉ"**.
4. Switch sort from "NAJRELEVANTNEJŠIE" to **"ABECEDNE"** (alphabetical) — sheets are ordered
   by house number, so you page through the village.
5. Census sheets list every resident of a house with **birthplace, year of birth, and
   *domovská príslušnosť* (domicile/pertinence)** — the fields that establish CS citizenship.

Helper indexes if the portal search is fiddly:
- **cisarik.com** — "1930 census of all villages in Slovakia," organized by village.
  → https://www.cisarik.com/census-1930-my-resources.html
- **poosch.sk** guide (English) with location list & download walkthrough.
  → https://poosch.sk/en/slovakiana-guides-download.html

### If the 1930 sheet isn't enough (belt-and-suspenders, by mail)
For the **1921 census**, unredacted **1939/1940** sheets, a *domovský list*, or anything not
digitized, request directly from the archive:
- **Štátny archív v Prešove, pracovisko Archív Svidník** — the regional archive for the Svidník
  district. Email correspondence, in Slovak.
- EHRI institution record: https://portal.ehri-project.eu/institutions/sk-003283
- Contact used in the handoff: **archiv.presov@minv.sk**
- Ask for: *sčítacie hárky (1921/1930), domovský list, or any domicile/citizenship record for
  the Meller family of Vyšná Pisaná.*

### The Jewish-records angle (Meller is a Jewish surname; "Shija" a Yiddish given name)
- Pre-1895 and alongside civil registration, births were also recorded in **Jewish
  congregation registers**. FamilySearch holds **Slovak synagogue books 1592–1935**, including
  Jewish congregations, with images from the Prešov archive.
- **JewishGen** indexes Sáros-county Jewish vital records; towns in the **1869 Sáros census**
  appear across 8 films — a good starting point for the Meller line.
  - Slovak civil vital records intro: https://www.jewishgen.org/databases/Hungary/SlovakCivilVitalRecords_intro.html
  - Sáros Jews guide: https://www.jewishgen.org/hungary/Data/Saros/1869%20SAROS%20JEWS%20in%20Towns.htm
  - Vivian Kahn's *Jewish Family Research in Slovakia* guide (PDF):
    https://www.jewishgen.org/hungary/slovak%20resources%20guide%20rev.pdf

## TASK 4 — Finishing steps (assembly & submission)

### Apostilles
- **State-issued vital records** → apostille from the **issuing state's Secretary of State**.
- **Federal documents** (FBI check) → apostille from the **US Department of State**.
- **NYC pre-1949 record apostille path (per-document):** Municipal Archives letter **$5** →
  **NY County Clerk** certification **$3** → **NY Dept. of State** apostille **$10**.
  With the $11 record that's ≈ **$29 all-in** per pre-1949 certificate.
- NY State route: [dos.ny.gov apostille](https://dos.ny.gov/apostille-certificate-authentication) ·
  [health.ny.gov vital-records apostille](https://www.health.ny.gov/vital_records/apostille.htm)

### Official (sworn) Slovak translation
- Must be done by a **súdny prekladateľ** — a translator **registered by the Slovak Ministry
  of Justice**. **Informal translations are not accepted.** This includes an official
  re-translation of the 1905 birth record. (The affidavit substitutes for the six
  tax/employment/insurance confirmations — use the consulate's form.)

### FBI Identity History Summary (background check)
- Two routes: **direct FBI** ($18, ~12–14 weeks) or an **FBI-approved channeler** (~3–5
  business days). Request the **physical original with FBI seal**, not a digital copy.
- Apostilled by the **US Department of State** (federal).
- Most receiving authorities accept it if **dated within 6 months** — so **order it only after
  the appointment date is set**, to avoid it expiring against the apostille/translation lead time.

### Appointment & consulate
- **Consulate General of the Slovak Republic in New York** — **+1 212-286-8434** — covers NY, NJ,
  PA, OH, MI, IL, MA, CT, and most of the NE/Midwest.
- **Appointment lead time ≈ 6–12 months** (2026). Book the date first; then trigger the FBI check.

### Fees & the 2026 law change
- **Government fee: €1,000** for a **great-grandparent** claim (this case). (€30 only if the
  qualifying ancestor is a parent/grandparent.)
- **Since 15 July 2026**, the **residency requirement is abolished** — no need to obtain Slovak
  residence before applying.
- Applicants **14+** must supply the full apostilled + officially-translated lineage.
