The read

Birth-in-territory is solid. Two things still to nail: the identity bridge (Shija Meller = Sam Miller — Task 1) and, as insurance, a bulletproof citizenship record from the Slovak archive (Task 2). The US immigration records may already satisfy the consulate's criteria, so Task 2 is belt-and-suspenders, not a blocker.

What the research turned up

  • Sam left in 1922, so he is NOT in the 1930 census. The census that lists him is the 1921 census (15 Feb 1921) — but that's archive-held, not the free online set. His Czechoslovak citizenship rests on domovské právo (domicile right) in Vyšná Pisaná, acquired 1918–1920 under Act 236/1920 and kept until his ~1928 US naturalization. Best proof: a domovský list, an old Czechoslovak passport (he likely emigrated on one), or the 1921 census — all via the archive (Task 2).
  • The free 1930 census still helps indirectly: search Slovakiana.sk for his father Meller Pál / relatives who stayed — it documents the family's pertinency in the village.
  • Fee flag: this is a great-grandparent claim → €1,000 government fee (€30 applies only to a parent/grandparent ancestor).
  • Law change, 15 Jul 2026: the prior residency requirement was abolished. And since summer 2025 the Ministry grants descent where the ancestor was alive after 1918 even if emigrated — Sam (b. 1904, emigrated 1922) fits cleanly.
  • Village names to search under: Vyšná Pisaná = Hungarian Felsőhímes (until 1899 Felső-Piszana), historically Sáros County — pre-1918 records are Hungarian-administered. The "1905 record / b. 31 Dec 1904" gap is just normal registration lag, not a contradiction.
